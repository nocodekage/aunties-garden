01 — Pen and washSumi-e

Take the colour away and the border is only shapes.

The same planting, laid down in ink. A hard pen line where a leaf crosses the sky, a loose grey wash everywhere else, and the paper left to do the rest.

What survives the loss of colour is structure: the cone of the blue spruce, the flat tiers of the dogwood, the vertical rhythm of trunks going back into the wood. It turns out she was composing all along.

02 — Silver edgeMoonlight

At night the moon only draws the outlines.

A low cold key from behind the wood, and every needle, leaf and stem picks up a thin bright rim where it turns away from you.

Nothing is lit, exactly. The garden goes almost black and comes back as a diagram of itself — the spruce a silver cone, the hostas a scatter of edged spoons, the trunks ruled straight up out of the dark.

03 — Last lightSunset

Everything else goes soft, and the cherry holds focus.

The garden falls out of focus on both sides of the tree. The petals come down slowly. Underneath it, the two of them are still reading, and neither has noticed the light going.

A real depth-of-field pass, driven off the scene's depth buffer — the blur is genuinely a function of how far each thing sits from the trunk, so walking forward pulls the border in and out of focus the way a lens would.
